The Chemical Brothers — Go

2015

Netflix's thriller Apex is the reason a decade-old Chemical Brothers track is currently sitting at number one on Shazam and dominating streaming charts globally. The film's director Baltasar Kormákur told Decider via Billboard that star Taron Egerton personally sourced the track for a key scene — one in which a character puts on a song and promises to give chase once it ends — and the placement turned out to be a near-perfect union of tension and sound. U.S. on-demand streams jumped from 92,000 to 487,000 in a single week, with daily plays peaking at 127,000 on April 30, according to Music Times. The clip from that scene has accumulated tens of millions of views across TikTok and Instagram, per We Rave You. Originally released as the second single from Born in the Echoes — featuring uncredited vocals from Q-Tip — "Go" earned a Grammy nomination in 2015 before settling into cult status. The 429% streaming surge, reported by Ace Showbiz, has also pushed it to number five on the Dance Digital Song Sales chart, a commercial lane the track never cracked the first time around.

Justin Bieber, Nicki Minaj — Beauty And A Beat

2012

Bieber's buzzy Coachella performances have sent a 13-year-old club track to the top of the global charts. According to Billboard, "Beauty and a Beat" is currently No. 1 on both the Billboard Global 200 and the Billboard Global Excl. U.S. chart, while a Threads post tracking Spotify data reported 7.43 million streams in a single day after the Coachella performance aired. The event generated an additional subplot: per AOL, Bieber cut the track before Nicki Minaj's verse, which produced over 200,000 TikTok posts in three days and a public response from Minaj herself — a side story that kept the song in feeds well after the set ended. Forbes notes the song only entered these rankings the week prior, making the jump to No. 1 unusually rapid for catalog material. The last non-holiday catalog track to reach No. 1 on the Global Excl. U.S. was Kate Bush's "Running Up That Hill" in 2022, driven by its Stranger Things sync — a very different kind of cultural ignition than a live performance.

Michael Jackson — Billie Jean

1982

The Michael biopic has triggered the largest streaming week of Michael Jackson's catalog in recorded history, and Billie Jean is leading the charge. According to Billboard, the track is sitting at No. 5 on the Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs chart, and per That Grape Juice, it has climbed to No. 38 on the Hot 100. In the UK, Forbes reports it has surged to a new peak on the Official Streaming chart while also re-entering the Official Singles tally. The song is now appearing broadly across charts in well over a hundred countries. The biopic effect on catalog is a well-documented phenomenon — Bohemian Rhapsody and Rocketman both produced multi-week streaming surges for their subjects — but the scale here, with ten Jackson titles simultaneously on the Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs chart, places this in a different tier.

Foo Fighters — Iron Rooster

2015
Have you ever been dumb enough to do what you wanted to do
Without good reason coming over you?
Have you ever been young enough to feel what you wanted to feel?
Take back those years for something real

"Iron Rooster" is broadly charting today on the strength of a live revival that has made one of the rarest tracks in the Foo Fighters catalog suddenly visible again. According to Billboard, the band performed the song at a recent underplay in New York — only the fourth time this year, and just the eighth time ever, following a 25-year performance hiatus. That kind of concentrated scarcity tends to push catalog curiosity hard: listeners who caught word of the show go looking for the recording.

The track originally appeared on the Saint Cecilia EP, released free in November 2015 as a goodwill gesture to fans. No specific film or TV placement for this song has surfaced in available reporting. What's driving the current chart presence appears to be the live-show story alone — a rare enough event that it functions as its own news cycle. For a deep cut that spent a decade largely unplayed, eight performances in a single year amounts to a second life.

Bethel Music, Amanda Cook — I Belong to You (Live)

2014

A live worship recording released in 2014 is appearing on streaming charts for the first time, across 144 country and platform cells simultaneously. The track appears on Spotify credited to Bethel Music and vocalist Amanda Cook, who has been a central figure in the Redding, California-based worship collective's catalog. Bethel Music occupies an unusual position in the streaming landscape — producing music built for congregational use that periodically crosses into general listening charts without conventional promotion. The dossier does not identify a specific driver for the current spike. Twelve years between release and first chart appearance is an outlier even by catalog-revival standards; the breadth of today's debut — 144 cells simultaneously — suggests the movement is not confined to a single region or platform.
