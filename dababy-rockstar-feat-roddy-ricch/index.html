The guitar-driven hit that dominated the summer of 2020 is back across the global charts, appearing on 46 today after a five-week absence. Built around an acoustic riff and a hook from a then-ascendant Roddy Ricch, the song spent seven non-consecutive weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100, per Wikipedia, and topped charts in the UK — DaBaby's highest commercial peak. Its protest-era resonance came partly from a Black Lives Matter–themed BET Awards performance the year of release. Rolling Stone Australia called it the pair "at the height of their powers." The track's softer register — DaBaby trading his signature bark for something closer to tender — has kept it durable. It re-enters as a steady catalog presence rather than a fresh spike.
