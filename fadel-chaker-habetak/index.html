A romantic ballad from one of Lebanon's most storied — and most controversial — voices is broadly charting again today, tracked across 133 country and platform charts after a six-week absence from the rankings. Fadel Chaker was among the Arab world's defining pop tenors of the 2000s before a dramatic withdrawal from public life; Habetak belongs to the run of singles he released remotely in the late 2010s, his voice still intact, his circumstances still unresolved. The song trades on that mystique: a slow, swelling arrangement built entirely around the grain of his vocal, the kind of full-throated lament that defines the Khaleeji-Levantine ballad tradition. Its current spread across the global charts marks a steady return to wide rotation rather than a flicker.
